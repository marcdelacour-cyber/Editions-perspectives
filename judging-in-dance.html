The book

Judging in Dance

What really happens when we observe, compare and decide.

A book by Marc Delacour about the human mechanisms involved in dance judging: attention, perception, emotions, fatigue, experience, preferences, cognitive biases and the influence of context.

Understanding judging does not mean imposing one way of judging. It means becoming more aware of what influences our perception and our decisions.

Why this book?

Competent and fair-minded adjudicators may observe the same performance and reach different conclusions. These differences do not always result from error or arbitrary decisions. They may also come from the normal way human attention, perception and decision-making work.

Making the mechanisms easier to understand

The book gives words to processes that are often experienced without being clearly identified: what attracts attention, what is remembered, how comparisons are made and what guides a decision.

Moving beyond simple oppositions

It helps readers move beyond labels such as “good judge” or “bad judge”, “objective” or “subjective”, in order to understand the real complexity of judging in a specific situation.

What you will find in the book

  • The limits of attention and selective perception;
  • The role of emotions, fatigue and context;
  • Artistic sensitivity and personal preferences;
  • The cognitive biases most often involved in judging;
  • Intuitive and analytical thinking, and the role of expertise.
  • The adjudicator’s inner dialogue and personal routines;
  • Group influence and the role of the panel;
  • How dancers, coaches and audiences perceive judging;
  • Reputation, appearance and the halo effect;
  • Ways to develop greater awareness and improve personal practice.

Who is the book for?

The book can be read from several points of view, depending on the reader’s role in dance.

Adjudicators and panel chairs

To question their own perception, explain their decisions more clearly and recognise the conditions in which judging takes place.

Dancers, couples and coaches

To understand more clearly how a performance may be perceived, compared and interpreted by different adjudicators.

Teachers, organisers and audiences

To move beyond a purely emotional or suspicious interpretation of results and better understand the role of the panel.

Two complementary perspectives

The book combines a scientific perspective on the mechanisms of judging with experience gained at the highest level of dance.

Patrick Lemaire highlights the richness, relevance and accessibility of the psychological knowledge used to understand the activity of judging.

A book that continues through dialogue

A book cannot answer every personal situation faced by an adjudicator, dancer or coach. The Judge’s Assistant can be used to ask a question, clarify a concept, examine a real situation or continue a reflection based on the content of the book.

It is multilingual and replies in the language of the question. It does not replace regulations, institutional frameworks or personal judgement.
